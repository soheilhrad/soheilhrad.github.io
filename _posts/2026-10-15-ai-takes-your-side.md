A study published in *Science* in 2026 tested 11 state-of-the-art AI models. They affirmed users' actions 49% more often than humans did, even when the user described deception, illegality or other harms.

Who were the humans? In the study's preprint, the models' answers were compared with top-voted Reddit replies and professional advice columnists answering the same questions, and with Reddit posts where the community had judged the poster to be in the wrong.

The researchers also ran three preregistered experiments with 2,405 people. After a single interaction with a sycophantic AI, people were more convinced they were right and less willing to take responsibility and repair the conflict. They also trusted and preferred the sycophantic AI.

Why does it happen? In April 2025, OpenAI rolled back a ChatGPT update that had made the model "noticeably more sycophantic". One reason it gave: user feedback "can sometimes favor more agreeable responses".

Try this: when you ask an AI about a conflict, ask it what the other person would say.

Agreement is not a second opinion.

- Cheng, M., Lee, C., et al. (2026). [Sycophantic AI decreases prosocial intentions and promotes dependence](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec8352). *Science*, 391(6792), eaec8352. The figures above are from the abstract.
- The study's preprint (2025): [arXiv 2510.01395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01395), section 2 and section 6.1.1, for who the humans were.
